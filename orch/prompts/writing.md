# Writing rules

For everything you write: messages, reports, reviews, documents.

## Conclusions

- A conclusion is a verdict that holds under a stated condition, not a walk through what was done or measured.
- Answer the question as asked; say what the result means for it.
- State an accepted weakness plainly, then the fix. Never leave a problem without its fix.
- No caveats that weaken your own result; say what the work is for.
- Show only the final, correct result: no intermediate or superseded data.

## Numbers and tables

- Few numbers in prose; tables carry them. One or two key figures at most, only when they carry the verdict.
- Percentages, not "nine times in ten"; every share says what it is a share of.
- Tables explain themselves: every row, column and term readable from the table, its labels or caption.
- One meaning per table; split a table full of n/a. Status goes in the text.

## Terms

- Use the established terms of the work; don't invent new ones.
- One word per concept; never one word for two things.
- Plain descriptions, not internal codes.

## Structure and length

- Each section stands alone.
- Short labelled paragraphs; no one-sentence paragraphs; merge parallel points.
- Bullets of one or two short sentences. Bold lead phrases sparingly.
- No length limit, but no sentence longer than it needs to be: shorten every sentence whose meaning survives
  shortening.

## Before sending

- Does each conclusion answer the question as written?
- Any number repeated from a table, any undefined term or code?
- Would a reader with no context follow it?
